Who we are

Mid-sized on purpose

Big enough that you are not dependent on one person’s calendar. Small enough that your account is not handed to whoever is free this week.

Our clients are established businesses with real operational systems: trades and construction, professional services, wholesale and logistics, community organisations, and suppliers to the resource sector. They tend to have the same complaints — systems that do not talk to each other, reporting nobody trusts, and an IT arrangement nobody owns.

We are not a reseller looking for something to sell you. We are the people who will look at what you have, tell you what is worth keeping, and fix what is not.

Our clients are not looking for a technology partner to be interesting. They want the systems to work, the invoice to match the quote, and someone to pick up the phone. That is a low bar that a lot of suppliers still miss — usually because nobody wrote anything down.

We started in 2016, which means we have watched a full generation of “game-changing” platforms arrive, get acquired, get sunset, and leave customers stranded. We choose tools we can support, document and hand over — and we assume the person maintaining this in three years will not be us.

That is also why every engagement produces documentation, credentials and a written record of decisions. A supplier who can be replaced easily is a supplier who has to keep earning the work.

What we stand for

Six rules we work to

These are the things we will not trade away, even when a job is on the line.

P01

Tell the truth about the job

If a $2,000 fix solves it, we will not sell you a $40,000 project. We would rather have your next three jobs than oversell this one.

P02

Write it down

Scopes, decisions, credentials, how the system works and why. Knowledge that lives in one person’s head is a risk you are paying for.

P03

Secure by default

MFA, least privilege, patching and tested backups are the starting point — not an upsell at the end of the project.

P04

Hand over properly

Documentation, training and access belong to you. A client who could leave is a client who stays.

P05

One name, one number

You deal with the people doing the work. No account managers relaying messages between you and your own project.

P06

Fix the boring things first

Backups, access and monitoring before new features. The unglamorous work is what keeps the business running.

If we do our job well, you stop thinking about IT. That is the actual product — not a portal, not a report, not a monthly meeting.

What we have learned

Six things this job teaches you

None of these are clever. All of them are cheaper to deal with before they cost you a week.

L01

Nobody buys documentation

And everybody needs it the week after the person who built the system goes on leave.

L02

The last 10% is the job

Reporting, exceptions, edge cases and training — the part that decides whether a system actually gets used.

L03

Shared logins are a fuse

One password in three hands means no audit trail and no clean way to remove access.

L04

An untested backup is an assumption

The only number that matters is how long a real restore takes.

L05

Reports nobody opens are decoration

If a report does not change a decision, it is overhead with a schedule.

L06

Cheap now is expensive in the data

Skipping structure on the way in costs multiples at the first integration.
